Read before drawing conclusions

  • Selection bias dominates. The schools at the top admit students who were already on a strong trajectory. The numbers reflect who they enroll as much as what they teach.
  • Data is partial. Most public schools don't publish per-college matriculation. Rows are tagged M (measured), E (estimated), R (rough), or N (no data) with explicit confidence intervals.
  • R-tier rows can be off by ±10 percentage points. Use rankings as a starting point for due diligence, not a final answer.
  • "T25" is fuzzy and excludes liberal arts colleges. Bay Area students disproportionately attend UC Berkeley/UCLA/Stanford — high T25 share partly reflects geography.
  • School culture matters. The Palo Alto teen-suicide cluster is documented; optimizing purely on matriculation points toward the highest-pressure schools.
