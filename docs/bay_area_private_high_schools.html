Bay Area Private High Schools — Top‑25 University Matriculation

A research snapshot focused on the past 4–5 graduating classes (~2020/21 → 2024/25). Compiled from publicly available school profiles, college counseling pages, and PDFs published for college admissions offices.

What this is: a decision aid for choosing among Bay Area independent & Catholic high schools, focused on outcomes at U.S. News Top‑25 National Universities. What it is not: a quality ranking. Selection bias is the dominant factor — see Methodology & caveats.
Data limitations are significant. Most Bay Area privates publish a 3–5 year cumulative list of where graduates have matriculated, but only some publish per‑college counts. Schools that publish only an alphabetical list (e.g., Sacred Heart Prep, Crystal Springs Uplands, Convent & Stuart Hall) are flagged "list only — counts not disclosed." Counts shown are reported by the schools themselves and have not been independently audited.

Consolidated "T25 %" ranking (publics + privates, top 25)

Single-number summary so schools can be ranked side-by-side. T25 % = the average % of a graduating class that matriculates (not just is admitted) to a U.S. News Top-25 National University, averaged over the most recent 3-5 graduating classes. UC Berkeley + UCLA are in the T25 set; LACs are not. The GS column is the GreatSchools 1-10 score (mostly "—" for privates, which GreatSchools rarely rates). The Niche column is the niche.com letter grade (A+ to D-). Confidence tags (M / E / R / N) reflect how strong the underlying T25 data is — see "T25 % methodology" below.

This is a transparent heuristic anchored to schools with published data, not a trained ML model. Confidence tags communicate uncertainty: estimates flagged R could easily be off by ±10pp; N means no usable data. Use the rank as a starting point for your own due diligence, not a final answer.

T25 % methodology

The new T25 % column collapses each row into one number so schools can be ranked side-by-side. It is a transparent heuristic, not a trained model. Estimates are anchored to the small set of schools that publish per-college matriculation counts; everything else is extrapolated from those anchors.

The estimator (4 steps)

  1. Anchor schools (tag M = Measured). For schools that publish per-college matriculation counts (Harker, Menlo, Nueva, College Prep, Marin Academy, Urban in this file; Paly and Gunn in the public-schools file), T25 % = (sum of matriculants to T25 schools per year) / (class size) × 100, averaged over the most recent 3-5 years available. UC Berkeley and UCLA count as T25; LACs do not. Recomputed from the cells above.
  2. Strong-proxy estimates (tag E = Estimated). For schools without per-college counts but with solid proxies (Niche A+, known reputation, comparable peer data, hits-every-T25 list, UC source-school CSV references), the band is set by analogy to the nearest anchor. Examples: SHP / Crystal Springs / Branson ≈ Menlo-tier (independent Peninsula/Marin); UHS / Lick-Wilmerding ≈ UHS-tier SF independents; Castilleja ≈ small selective all-girls; Bellarmine / Mitty / SI / Saint Francis = large Catholic schools where T25 % is structurally lower than independents because of college-preference patterns (Notre Dame yes, but also Santa Clara, USF, USD which aren’t in the T25).
  3. Rough estimates (tag R = Rough). For schools with only Niche-grade or sparse proxies, we use Niche-grade-to-T25 % bands: A+ elite (top 50 nationally) 15-30%; A+ solid 8-18%; A 4-10%; A- 2-6%; B+ 1-3%; below 1%. Adjusted upward for affluent district / very small selective class, and downward for class size dilution / mostly-CC-bound demographics.
  4. Sanity checks. Cap at the highest anchor (~48% from CPS Oakland); floor at 0.5% for plausible college-going schools; conservative for very large classes; cross-check schools in the same district/demographic should be in the same band ±5%; Catholic schools systematically lower than secular peers because of college-preference patterns.
This is a transparent heuristic anchored to schools with published data, not a trained ML model. Estimates flagged R could be off by ±10 percentage points. Use the rank as a starting point for your own due diligence, not a final answer.

GS & Niche columns

The GS column is the school's GreatSchools.org 1-10 summary score. GreatSchools rates almost exclusively public schools using state test & outcome data, so the column is "—" for the overwhelming majority of privates in this file. The Niche column is the niche.com overall letter grade (A+ down to D-), which aggregates academics, teachers, college prep, diversity, and parent/student survey data and does cover most privates. Treat both as third-party reputational signals, not as independent measures of T25 outcomes — they use very different inputs from the T25 % column and should be read alongside, not instead of, the matriculation evidence. Grades shown are based on training-data knowledge of well-known schools as of early 2026; for less-covered schools we leave the cell as "—" rather than guess.

Methodology & caveats

How this was built

  • Each school's own school-profile PDF or college-counseling webpage was the primary source. Where the school publishes a multi-year matriculation list with per-college counts (Harker, Menlo, College Prep, Marin Academy, Urban, Castilleja, Athenian, Pinewood, Nueva), data confidence is highest.
  • Where the school publishes only an alphabetical list with "bold = matriculated" (Sacred Heart Prep, Crystal Springs Uplands, Sonoma Academy, San Domenico, Pinewood, NDB, Bentley), we record presence but not counts.
  • For schools with no public matriculation document (most Catholic large-enrollment schools, plus several small SF schools), we either flag as EST using Niche.com aggregates or leave the cell explicit.
  • T25 reference list = U.S. News National Universities Top 25 as of approximately the 2024–2025 cycle. The composition shifts slightly year to year (e.g., Wake Forest, Tufts, NYU, USC sometimes in/near). Excludes liberal arts colleges (Williams, Amherst, Pomona, Swarthmore, etc.) — which would dramatically change the picture for several schools (Marin Academy, College Prep, Castilleja in particular).

Why "best matriculation" ≠ "best fit"

  • Selection bias dominates. Castilleja, Harker, Nueva, UHS, College Prep, Crystal Springs, Sacred Heart Prep, Branson, Menlo, and Marin Academy all admit ~15–30% of applicants. Their T25 numbers reflect the kids they admit at least as much as anything they teach.
  • Acceptance vs. matriculation. Some lists conflate them. "Bentley accepted to T25 X" ≠ "Bentley sent N students to T25 X."
  • Year-to-year variance. A class of 50 kids will have wild swings (3 to Stanford one year, 0 the next). 5-year cumulative numbers smooth this out — single-year claims should be ignored.
  • Missing the LAC story. Many of the most "elite" Bay Area kids go to Pomona, Williams, Swarthmore, Amherst, Harvey Mudd, Wellesley, etc. Those don't show up in T25 National Universities counts. College Prep, Castilleja, and Marin Academy all send substantial cohorts to top LACs.
  • Catholic schools deserve different metrics. Bellarmine, Mitty, Saint Francis, SI, SHC, De La Salle each enroll 300–500 graduates per class. They don't optimize for HYPSM and largely don't publish counts. Their T25 share looks small in % terms but absolute Stanford / Berkeley admits per year is competitive.
  • Single-sex effects. Castilleja, Mercy, NDB, Presentation (girls); Bellarmine, De La Salle, Stuart Hall (boys). Family/student fit considerations beyond outcomes data.
  • Test policies. Several schools (Marin Academy, Castilleja, BASIS, etc.) have explicit notes about Bay Area test-center scarcity. Many graduates apply test-optional. Reported SATs may overstate actual class profile.
  • Value-add observation: the schools that punch most above their selectivity (i.e., produce more T25 outcomes than their admit rate would suggest) tend to be the ones with very strong, dedicated college counseling staffs (College Prep, Crystal Springs, Castilleja). Eastside College Prep is the singular value-add story — getting first-gen / low-income students from East Palo Alto into Stanford, Princeton, Yale, MIT.
Practical advice for an attendance decision:
  1. If your child is a top STEM kid and you want maximum HYPSM optionality: Harker, Nueva, Castilleja (girls), BASIS Independent SV, Crystal Springs Uplands.
  2. If you want the broadest selective-college pipeline (T25 + LACs): Menlo, Sacred Heart Prep, Marin Academy, College Prep, Branson.
  3. If a Catholic / values-based environment matters and you can accept a less concentrated T25 outcome: SI, SHC, Bellarmine, Mitty, Saint Francis, plus Sacred Heart Prep or SHC at the higher selectivity end.
  4. If you want creative / progressive / arts-leaning culture: Urban, Lick-Wilmerding, Marin Academy, Athenian, Drew.
  5. If price matters significantly: Catholic schools (~$24–32k) cost roughly half of independents (~$55–68k). Eastside College Prep is tuition-free but selective on socioeconomic grounds.
Compiled April 2026 from publicly available school profile documents, college-counseling pages, and public matriculation lists. All figures are as reported by the schools themselves and have not been independently verified. Tuition figures are approximate and rise ~5%/year. T25 reference list is the U.S. News National Universities Top 25 as of approximately the 2024–2025 ranking cycle. This document is a research aid, not a recommendation.
