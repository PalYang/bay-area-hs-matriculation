How T50 % is estimated

This page explains how the T50 % column on the schools tables is computed (for M-anchor schools) or estimated (for everyone else).

1. Definition

T50 % = % of the graduating class that matriculates to a U.S. News Top-50 National University. T50 includes all of T25 plus the next 25 schools. The full additional T26-50 set (rough — boundary shifts year to year):

USC, NYU, Tufts, UC Santa Barbara, UC San Diego, UC Irvine, UC Davis, Boston College, Boston University, Tulane, Wake Forest, Lehigh, U Rochester, Brandeis, Case Western, William & Mary, Northeastern, Georgia Tech, U Wisconsin–Madison, UIUC (Illinois), UT Austin, Pepperdine, U Florida, Yeshiva, Villanova.

UCSD, UCI, UCD are in T50 but not T25 — important for Bay Area schools whose UC matriculation spreads beyond UCB/UCLA.

2. Why T50 % matters separately from T25 %

T25 cuts at #25, which excludes major Bay Area destinations:

  • USC, NYU, BU — common alternatives to T25 for Bay Area students who want a city-private experience
  • UCSD, UCI, UCD — UC-eligible students who don't get UCB/UCLA
  • Northeastern, BC, Tufts — common East Coast options below the T25 line

For most Bay Area schools, T50 % captures roughly 50% more matriculation than T25 % — a school with 20% T25 typically has 32–40% T50.

3. M-anchor calculation

For schools with published per-college matriculation lists, T50 % is computed by summing matriculation counts to all colleges in the T50 set, dividing by class size, and averaging across the published years.

Worked example: College Preparatory School (Oakland)

CPS 5-year cumulative matriculations to T26-50 schools (approximate, from CPS published list):
  USC 8, NYU 6, Tufts 5, BC 3, BU 4, Northeastern 5, GA Tech 2, U Wisconsin 1, UCSB 4, UCSD 6, UCI 2, UCD 3, others ~10
  Sum T26-50 ≈ 59 over 5 yrs = ~12/yr
  Plus T25 of ~38/yr (= 47.8% × 80 grads)
  T50 % = (38 + 12) / 80 ≈ 62.5%

Note that CPS's actual T50 % is higher than its T25 % by about 15 percentage points — the spread is broader than the headline T25 % suggests.

Why the ratio is highest for mid- and Catholic schools. Catholic and mid-tier publics have many students whose top-target college is in the T26-50 range (USC, BC, Tulane for Catholic; UCSD, UCI, UCD for publics). Their T25 % is small but their T50 % is much larger. Elite privates already place most strong students at T25, so T26-50 just adds the long tail.

5. Known sources of error

  • Boundary fuzziness. #50 vs #51 is a coin flip in U.S. News methodology. A school where Villanova or Wake Forest ranks just above #50 vs just below shifts T50 % by 1–3 percentage points without any underlying change in school behavior.
  • UC-rich Bay Area effect. UCSD/UCI/UCD are massive matriculation destinations for Bay Area public-school cohorts. The T50 % for top publics is heavily UCSD/UCI/UCD-driven, more so than national-comparable schools.
  • Catholic schools' "Catholic T26-50." Notre Dame is T10, but BC, Villanova, Loyola Marymount (T51-100), Santa Clara (T51-100) drive Catholic matriculation. The T26-50 jump is especially uncertain for Catholic schools because the BC/Villanova share fluctuates year to year.
  • Estimate stack. Non-M T50 % = ratio × estimated T25 %. Both are estimates; uncertainty stacks.

6. Confidence tags carry over

Same as T10 and T25 — the conf tag (M / E / R / N) applies to all metrics. An M-anchor T50 % was computed from published data; non-M T50 % inherits the T25 % uncertainty and adds ~2pp from the ratio approximation.
