How T10 % is estimated

This page explains how the T10 % column on the schools tables is computed (for M-anchor schools) or estimated (for everyone else).

1. Definition

T10 % = % of the graduating class that matriculates to a U.S. News Top-10 National University. The T10 set:

  1. Princeton
  2. MIT
  3. Harvard
  4. Stanford
  5. Yale
  6. Caltech
  7. Duke
  8. Johns Hopkins
  9. Northwestern
  10. Penn

List current as of the U.S. News 2024–2025 ranking cycle. Ties at the boundary slightly expand the set (e.g., a year where Penn ties with Brown could push #11 into the bucket). Matriculation, not acceptance — where students actually enroll.

2. Why T10 % matters separately from T25 %

T25 % bundles HYPSM with UCB, UCLA, Michigan, UVA, Notre Dame, etc. A school can be "20% T25" while sending almost no one to HYPSM — they just have a strong UC pipeline. T10 % strips out that effect.

The gap between T10 % and T25 % is the cleanest single signal of whether a school's outcomes lean ultra-elite-private vs strong-state-flagship.

3. M-anchor calculation (schools with published per-college lists)

For these ~10 schools, T10 % is computed directly from the school's published matriculation list. Sum matriculations to the 10 schools listed above, divide by class size, average across the years in the published window.

Worked example: Henry M. Gunn HS

Gunn 2024 matriculations to T10 schools (per Gunn's CCC page):
  Stanford 14, Harvard 4, Princeton 2, MIT 3, Yale 2, Northwestern 1, Penn 1, Duke 0, JHU 0, Caltech 1
  Sum = 28
  Class size = ~440
  T10 % = 28 / 440 ≈ 6.4%

Compare to Gunn's T25 % of 17.8% — about 36% of Gunn's T25 matriculation goes to T10, the other 64% goes to UCB/UCLA/UCSD/Michigan/CMU/etc.

4. Estimation for non-M schools

For schools without a published per-college list (E, R, N tags — about 145 schools), T10 % is estimated as a multiple of T25 % using the calibrated ratio table below:

School typeT10/T25 ratioReasoning
Elite private / national-feeding0.35–0.50Top-tier admissions; cohort qualifies for HYPSM admits at high rate. Anchored to Harker, Castilleja, CPS.
Top SF/Marin progressive private0.25–0.40Spread shifts to LACs and broader T25; Stanford remains accessible. Anchored to Urban, Lick-Wilmerding, Marin Academy.
Top public (Gunn-tier)0.20–0.35Strong cohort but T25 is UC-heavy. HYPSM admits exist but smaller share. Anchored to Gunn, Paly, Lynbrook.
Strong suburban public0.10–0.20Smaller HYPSM admit share; matriculation skews CSU/UC-mid.
Catholic college-prep0.08–0.15Lower HYPSM admit rate; Notre Dame is the main T10 destination, capping share.
Mid-tier public0.05–0.15HYPSM admits rare but not zero; usually 1–2 per class.
Lower-tier public / Title I0.0–0.10Mostly zero; equity-driven UCB or Stanford admits add 1–2 in good years.
Why the ratio varies. A school's T10/T25 ratio reflects how concentrated its top placements are at the very top of the list. Elite privates have admit-screened cohorts where many students could attend HYPSM. UC-pipeline publics have many strong students whose typical destination is UCB or UCLA — also T25, but not T10.

5. Known sources of error

  • Small-sample noise. A school with 80 graduates per year shows ±2–3 students of T10 variance from random year-to-year admit fluctuation. That's 2.5–4 percentage points — comparable to the entire estimate for some schools.
  • Stanford geography. Bay Area schools cluster near Stanford. Stanford comprises 30–60% of any Bay Area school's T10 matriculation. A school's relative Stanford yield drives its T10 % more than any other single factor.
  • Year-to-year variance for small private schools. A class with 1 fewer Harvard admit drops T10 % by 1pp on a 100-student class. Three-year averages help; single years can mislead.
  • Catholic schools' Notre Dame skew. Notre Dame is in T10 but is heavily over-represented in Catholic-school matriculation vs the rest of T10. SI / Bellarmine / Mitty estimates may overstate "ultra-elite" feeling — many of those T10 admits are Notre Dame, not HYPSM.

6. Confidence tags carry over

The conf tag (M / E / R / N) on each row applies to all metrics, not just T25. An M row's T10 % was computed from the same source data as its T25 % and carries the same confidence. An R row's T10 % is a ratio applied to an R-tier T25 % — its uncertainty stacks (roughly ±3pp).
