Bay Area Public High Schools — T25 University Matriculation

A research compilation focused on graduating classes ~2020–2025. Built for a family choosing where to attend high school based on Top-25 (US News National Universities) matriculation outcomes.

Scope. 9 Bay Area counties (San Francisco, San Mateo, Santa Clara, Alameda, Contra Costa, Marin, Sonoma, Napa, Solano), public schools only. Private schools (e.g., Harker, Menlo School, Castilleja, Crystal Springs Uplands, Bishop O'Dowd, Marin Academy, Branson, Sacred Heart, Bentley, Lick-Wilmerding, Stuart Hall, College Prep, Head-Royce) are explicitly excluded — handled by a sister analysis.

T25 reference list (US News National Universities, 2024–25 ranking, ties expanding the list). Princeton, MIT, Harvard, Stanford, Yale, Caltech, Duke, Johns Hopkins, Northwestern, Penn, Cornell, UChicago, Brown, Columbia, Dartmouth, UCLA, UC Berkeley, Notre Dame, Rice, Vanderbilt, Carnegie Mellon, Michigan, WashU, Emory, UVA, Georgetown.
READ THIS FIRST — DATA LIMITATIONS.
  • California public high schools do not centrally report college matriculation. Each school decides whether and how to publish it. Quality varies wildly.
  • Two of the three "elite four" (Mission San Jose, Monta Vista, Lynbrook) do NOT publish per-college matriculation counts publicly in the school profile PDFs we could access. We can confirm they are top UC feeders (UC Berkeley source data) but cannot list per-T25 counts with confidence.
  • The two schools with the cleanest, longest, year-by-year per-college data are Palo Alto High (Paly) and Henry M. Gunn HS — both publish full multi-year matriculation grids on their counseling websites. Their numbers are presented in full below.
  • "Matriculated" (enrolled) ≠ "Admitted." Many anecdotal lists (Niche, college-counselor blogs) mix these. Where ambiguous, we flag it.
  • Several PDF profiles we found are scanned/binary and the text could not be reliably extracted via webfetch. They are linked in the source column; a human can verify.
  • Selection bias warning: Mission San Jose, Monta Vista, Lynbrook, Saratoga, and Lowell are highly self-selected (Lowell is exam-admit; the others draw demographically homogeneous, academically intense student bodies). Their T25 rates reflect input as much as output.
  • Some 2025 numbers are from the most recent profile (which reports the previous June's class). Years labeled below are graduating-class years.

Top performers (with caveats)

Based on the data we could actually verify (with full awareness that some of the most-discussed schools have weak public data), here is what the evidence supports:

  1. Henry M. Gunn HS (Palo Alto) — Best verified T25 matriculation in the Bay Area. Publishes a clean year-by-year per-college table. ~400 students/grad class; in 2024 sent ~85+ students to T25 schools (Stanford 14, UC Berkeley 30, UCLA 10, plus strong showings at CMU, Cornell, Penn, Northwestern, Michigan, Emory, Brown, Columbia, UChicago). 2025: Stanford 22, Berkeley 19, UCLA 8, CMU 9, Cornell 6 — ~85+ T25 again.
  2. Palo Alto HS (Paly) — Same district, similar quality of data; ~470/class. 2024: Stanford 11, Berkeley 35, plus solid Northwestern (8), CMU (4), Cornell (1), MIT (2), Penn (2), Dartmouth (2). 2025: Stanford 12, Berkeley 23, Northwestern 6, Cornell 5, CMU 5. ~70+ T25 per year.
  3. Mission San Jose HS (Fremont) — Widely regarded as the top public for elite outcomes (#1 in chicardgo's ECPI). ~40 to UC Berkeley/year, ~40 to UCLA/year historically (UC source-school data). Caveat: per-T25 counts not in public profile — ranking is heavily based on triangulation and reputation.
  4. Monta Vista HS (Cupertino) — Confirmed top UC feeder (~204 to UC Berkeley over 2012–16; ~40/yr historically). Same caveat as MSJ — per-T25 counts not publicly itemized.
  5. Lowell HS (San Francisco) — Largest single feeder to UC system, especially Berkeley and Davis. Class size ~640. SFUSD's only academic-admission high school. Caveat: SFUSD profile PDF does not publish per-college matriculation counts; the strength is overwhelmingly UC-pipeline rather than Ivy/elite-private.

Honorable mentions with strong but not-perfectly-documented data: Saratoga HS (sends large cohorts T25; school profile lists aggregate categories rather than per-college counts); Lynbrook HS (peer of Monta Vista; same data limitation); Dougherty Valley HS (San Ramon, large strong cohort, profile mostly aggregate).

Methodology & caveats

What "T25" means here

This report uses the US News National Universities ranking roughly as of 2024–25, with ties expanding the list slightly: Princeton, MIT, Harvard, Stanford, Yale, Caltech, Duke, Johns Hopkins, Northwestern, Penn, Cornell, UChicago, Brown, Columbia, Dartmouth, UCLA, UC Berkeley, Notre Dame, Rice, Vanderbilt, Carnegie Mellon, Michigan, WashU, Emory, UVA, Georgetown. Rankings shift year to year — "Top 25" should be read as "the most selective tier of national research universities," not a precise cut-off.

How data was gathered

  • School-published College Matriculation Summary pages (Paly, Gunn — gold standard).
  • School Profile PDFs sent to colleges (most schools have these; quality of college-list section varies enormously).
  • The University of California's "Admissions by source school" data (authoritative for UC Berkeley/UCLA pipeline counts but lags).
  • Sequoia District schools' "College Destinations" pages (M-A, Carlmont, Woodside).
  • Triangulation against chicardgo.com's Bay Area Elite College Placement Index, Niche, and US News rankings.
  • Local press (Palo Alto Online, Patch, KRON) for occasional class-of-X feature stories.

Why so many "med" / "lo" rows?

California public high schools are not required to publish college matriculation outcomes, and most do not publish per-college counts in machine-readable form. Many post the data in PDFs that are scanned images or that defeated automated text extraction in this research session. The schools in high-confidence rows are those that chose to publish a clean year-by-year per-college table on a public page. That choice itself correlates with strong outcomes (those schools are proud of them).

Important biases to be aware of

  • Selection bias. Mission San Jose, Monta Vista, Lynbrook, Saratoga, and Lowell admit (or attract) demographically and academically narrow student bodies. Their T25 placement rates reflect who they enroll as much as what they teach. Sending a strong student to one of these schools does not guarantee they "level up" — they are surrounded by peers with similar profiles.
  • Self-reporting differences. Some schools count "enrolled," others count "admitted" or "applied." A school showing 35 to UC Berkeley likely means enrolled; a school showing 35 to "Ivies" might mean admits-anywhere.
  • Class size matters. Gunn (~440) sending 14 to Stanford is a much higher per-capita rate than Lowell (~640) or Berkeley HS (~800). Compare percentages where possible.
  • Recency. 2024 and 2025 data is preferred. Some schools' most recent accessible profile is 2019-20 or 2022-23. Treat older data as indicative, not current.
  • UC focus. Bay Area public outcomes lean very heavily on UC Berkeley, UCLA, UCSD, UCSB, etc. If you weight T25 toward private/Ivy, the rankings shift toward Gunn, Paly, M-A, Piedmont, Saratoga.
  • Counselor-to-student ratio matters for college outcomes. Mission San Jose, Lynbrook, Monta Vista have famously high ratios (300–400:1). Paly and Gunn have substantially better ratios.
  • Mental health context. Palo Alto schools have well-documented mental-health crises tied to academic pressure; Mission San Jose, Lynbrook, Monta Vista similar. T25 outcomes come at a cost worth weighing.

Key data gaps in this report

  • Per-T25 itemized counts for Mission San Jose, Monta Vista, Lynbrook, Cupertino, Homestead, Saratoga, Lowell — the most-discussed schools all have weaker public reporting than Paly/Gunn.
  • 5-year aggregates for any school — nobody publishes these as a single number.
  • Yield-adjusted data (admit vs. enroll).
  • Anything for the smaller / less-affluent districts (Sonoma, Solano, much of Napa).

If you want to do better than this

  • Email the College & Career Center at any school you're seriously considering. Many will share the latest matriculation list on request.
  • Download UC's "Admissions by source school" CSV for the most recent year (2024 enrollees) — that's the single most authoritative public dataset for UC Berkeley/UCLA pipeline counts.
  • Cross-check the linked PDFs in this report manually — many contain the per-college table but defeated automated extraction.
  • Naviance/SCOIR data (typically only available to enrolled families) gives the cleanest school-specific picture.
